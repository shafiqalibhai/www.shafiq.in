Self-Promotion at Work

15 May 2025 · 1 min · 213 words · Shafiq Alibhai

Self-promotion gets a bad reputation because people confuse it with boasting. Done well, it’s just making sure the right people know what you’ve done.

Use “I” when it’s your work#

If you led the project, say “I led the project.” Saving “we” for genuinely collaborative work means your individual contributions stand out when they matter.

Performance reviews are your moment#

If your organisation does annual reviews, prepare a list of accomplishments before the meeting. Don’t rely on your manager to remember everything you did over twelve months.

Speak up in meetings#

You don’t need to dominate every conversation. But if you have the answer or a useful perspective, say it. Silence reads as disengagement.

Make your boss look good#

Your manager’s manager is the person who decides your promotion. If your boss succeeds, you succeed. Share your results with your manager proactively so they have material to work with.

Build alliances#

Identify the people who influence decisions in your organisation and have regular one-to-ones with them. Not to curry favour — to understand their problems and see where your work intersects with theirs.

Avoid the obvious traps#

Don’t embarrass colleagues in public. Don’t take credit for other people’s work. Competence matters more than self-promotion in the long run, but competence alone isn’t enough.
