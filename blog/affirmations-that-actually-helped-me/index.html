Affirmations that actually helped me

Table of Contents
  1. The short ones
  2. The harder ones
  3. What I learned

I started doing affirmations roughly two years ago, after a particularly rough patch left me convinced I was fundamentally broken. Not in the dramatic sense – just that quiet, persistent voice in the back of my head telling me I wasn’t quite enough. You know the one.

The first time I tried it, I felt like an absolute idiot. Standing in front of the mirror, saying “I am worthy” out loud, while my brain screamed bullshit. I lasted about forty-five seconds before giving up.

But something kept pulling me back to it. Maybe because the voice never got quieter on its own. Maybe because I was desperate enough to try anything.

Here are the ones that actually stuck. Not the polished, Instagram-ready versions – the ones that felt true enough to repeat even when I didn’t fully believe them yet.

The short ones#

These are for the mornings when I can barely face the day:

“I am allowed to take up space.”

I spent most of my twenties making myself smaller. Less opinion, less presence, less me. This one hit differently because it wasn’t about adding something – it was about stopping the subtraction.

“This feeling is temporary.”

Not “this will get better.” Not “look on the bright side.” Just: temporary. It passed before, it’ll pass again. Simple enough to say when the anxiety is clawing at my ribs.

“I don’t have to earn my rest.”

The guilt of doing nothing used to keep me up at night. This one didn’t fix that overnight, but repeating it slowly eroded the belief that I needed to be productive to deserve peace.

The harder ones#

These are for the days when I’m actually feeling okay, and I want to protect that:

“I am not my mistakes.”

Easy to say. Harder to mean. I still catch myself folding past failures into my identity like they’re defining documents. But saying it out loud, even when it sounds naive, creates a little space between me and the shame.

“I trust myself to figure this out.”

Not that I’ll figure it out perfectly. Not that I won’t make it worse first. Just that I’ve survived every worst-case scenario so far, and I’ll probably survive this one too.

“I am allowed to change my mind.”

The pressure to be consistent – to stick to the plan, the opinion, the version of myself I presented last week – used to trap me in decisions I’d already outgrown. This one gave me permission to evolve without treating it like betrayal.

What I learned#

Affirmations don’t work because they’re magic. They work because repetition rewires attention. You can’t think yourself into a new belief in one sitting, but you can think your way there over enough sittings.

The ones that helped me weren’t the grandiose ones – “I am successful and powerful” felt like a joke when I was struggling to pay rent. They were the small, specific ones that addressed the actual thoughts running on loop in my head.

If you’re going to try this, skip the generic lists. Write your own. The ones that make you cringe a little are usually the ones you need most.
